Extended Warranties: Which Ones Are Actually Worth It

By Vrynt · May 29, 2026 · 7 min read

Extended warranties — officially called vehicle service contracts — are one of the most profitable products dealerships sell. That doesn't mean they're a bad deal for you. It means the dealer's price isn't your only option, and knowing which cars benefit from coverage versus which don't is the real decision.

When an Extended Warranty Makes Sense

Used luxury and European vehicles. A BMW, Mercedes, Audi, or Land Rover out of factory warranty can have repair bills that make your eyes water. A single transmission issue on a luxury car can run $4,000-$8,000. If you're buying one of these used, coverage is worth serious consideration.

Vehicles with known reliability issues. Some models have well-documented weak points — certain transmissions, turbo systems, or electronics. A quick search for "[model] common problems" tells you what to expect. If the list is long, coverage makes sense.

High-mileage daily drivers you plan to keep. If you're buying a car with 60K miles and plan to drive it to 150K, that's a lot of time for expensive components to fail.

When to Skip It

Toyota, Honda, and other high-reliability brands. These cars are engineered to run with minimal issues well past 200K miles. The odds of a major repair during a warranty period are low enough that the math doesn't favor coverage.

New cars with long factory warranties. If you're buying new with a 5-year/60K factory warranty and plan to trade in before it expires, an extended warranty covers nothing.

The Price Problem

The dealer might quote $2,500 for an extended warranty that costs them $400-$800 wholesale. This is where most people overpay. You have options: negotiate the dealer's price down (they have significant margin to work with), buy from a third-party provider online for a fraction of the dealer cost, or add coverage later — most plans can be purchased within a window after the sale.

If the dealer won't negotiate on the warranty price, telling them you'll think about it and potentially add it later often brings the price down. They'd rather sell it at a lower margin than lose the sale entirely.

The bottom line: Extended warranties aren't scams — they're insurance products with real value for certain vehicles. The question isn't whether coverage is worth having. It's whether the dealer's price is fair for what you're getting. Know your car's reliability track record, shop the price, and never buy out of pressure in the F&I office. You can almost always add it later at a better price.
