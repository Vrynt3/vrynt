Fidelity vs Vanguard vs Schwab: Which Brokerage Is Best?

By Vrynt · May 29, 2026 · 7 min read

The honest answer: for the vast majority of people, it doesn't matter. All three are excellent, all three offer low-cost index funds, and all three will serve you well for decades. Picking between them is like picking between three nearly identical good restaurants — the food is great everywhere, just pick one and eat.

That said, there are small differences that might tip the scale for your specific situation.

Fidelity

The best all-around option for most people. Zero-expense-ratio index funds (FZROX, FZILX) that literally charge nothing. Excellent app and website. Strong customer service. Fractional share trading. Cash management account with no fees and ATM reimbursements. If your 401(k) is through Fidelity (like mine), keeping your IRA there too simplifies everything.

Vanguard

The original home of index investing — Jack Bogle built the philosophy here. Their index funds (VTSAX, VTIAX) are the gold standard with razor-thin expense ratios. Vanguard's unique ownership structure means the company is literally owned by its fund shareholders, so their incentives are aligned with yours. The downside: their website and app feel dated compared to Fidelity and Schwab. It works, it's just not pretty.

Schwab

The best option if you want a traditional banking relationship alongside your investments. Schwab has physical branches, a strong checking account with no foreign transaction fees, and excellent customer service. Their index funds are competitive with Fidelity and Vanguard. If you want to walk into a branch and talk to someone, Schwab is the pick.

The Real Decision

If your 401(k) is at one of these three, open your IRA there too — simplicity has value. If you're starting fresh with no existing relationship, Fidelity is the slight edge for most people because of the zero-fee funds and best-in-class digital experience. But genuinely, you can't go wrong with any of them. The difference in expense ratios between these three is measured in hundredths of a percent. What matters infinitely more is that you actually open the account and start investing.

The bottom line: All three are excellent. Fidelity for the best app and zero-fee funds. Vanguard for the purest index investing philosophy. Schwab for physical branches and banking integration. Pick whichever matches your existing accounts or your personal preference, and stop overthinking it. The best brokerage is the one you actually use.
