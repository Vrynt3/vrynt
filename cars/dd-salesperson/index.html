🔧 Dealership Decoded

Why the Salesperson Isn't Your Enemy

By Vrynt · June 1, 2026 · 7 min read

The internet has trained car buyers to treat salespeople as adversaries. Walk in suspicious, reveal nothing, negotiate everything, trust no one. This approach might feel empowering, but it usually makes the experience worse for everyone — including you.

Here's what most buyers don't understand: the salesperson's commission on a typical car deal is shockingly small. On a $35,000 vehicle, a salesperson might earn $150-$400. They're not getting rich on your deal. Most salespeople earn the majority of their income from volume — selling lots of cars, not squeezing maximum profit from each one. They genuinely want the deal to move smoothly and quickly.

What Actually Happens Behind the Scenes

When you make an offer, the salesperson takes it to the sales manager. The salesperson doesn't set prices — they're a messenger and a guide. A good salesperson advocating for you to the manager is worth more than the $200 you might save by being adversarial. I've seen managers approve tighter deals because the salesperson said "this customer is great to work with — let's make it happen."

How to Get the Best Experience

Be upfront about what you want. If you've done your research and have a fair offer, say so. "I've looked at KBB and Edmunds, and I'd like to be in this range" — that's a salesperson's dream customer. They know the deal will close, they know the customer is informed, and they can focus on making it happen instead of playing games.

Treat them like a professional, because they are one. The ones who've been doing this for years know more about the car, the financing options, and the market than any website. That knowledge is free — use it.

The Decoded take: The salesperson makes a fraction of what you think they make, has less control over pricing than you assume, and can be your biggest advocate if you let them. Come prepared, be direct, and treat them like a human. The best deals I've seen happen when both sides work together, not against each other.
