Crypto Scams to Watch Out For (With Real Examples)

By Vrynt · May 29, 2026 · 8 min read

Crypto attracts scammers the way a gold rush attracts con artists. The combination of irreversible transactions, technical complexity, and emotional FOMO creates a perfect environment for fraud. Billions are lost every year — and most of it is preventable if you know what to look for.

The Most Common Scams

Fake Exchanges and Wallets

Scammers create convincing replicas of legitimate exchange websites and wallet apps. You deposit crypto and it's gone. Always double-check URLs, download apps only from official sources, and bookmark your exchange login page instead of Googling it each time (scammers buy Google ads that look like real results).

Impersonation and Social Engineering

Someone posing as Coinbase support, a crypto influencer, or even Elon Musk messages you offering to "double your Bitcoin" or "verify your wallet." No legitimate entity will ever ask for your seed phrase, private keys, or ask you to send crypto to receive more back. Period. If someone asks for your seed phrase, it's a scam 100% of the time.

Pump and Dump Schemes

A group buys large amounts of a tiny, low-volume token, hypes it on social media ("1000x gem!"), waits for retail investors to pile in and drive the price up, then dumps their holdings. The price crashes and latecomers lose everything. If a random token is being aggressively promoted in your DMs or a Telegram group, assume it's a pump and dump.

Rug Pulls

A team launches a new crypto project, collects millions in investment, and then disappears with the funds. Common in DeFi and NFT projects. Red flags: anonymous team, unrealistic promised returns, no audit of the smart contract, pressure to invest quickly.

Pig Butchering

A stranger befriends you online (dating apps, LinkedIn, social media), builds a relationship over weeks or months, then introduces you to a "great investment platform." You deposit money, see fake returns on a fake dashboard, deposit more, and eventually discover you can't withdraw. The platform was never real and the "friend" was a scammer (often working under coercion themselves).

Universal Red Flags

Guaranteed returns — no legitimate investment guarantees returns. Urgency and pressure — "act now or miss out" is manipulation, not opportunity. Requests for your seed phrase or private keys — always a scam. Unsolicited messages about crypto opportunities — always suspicious. Returns that sound too good to be true — they are.

The bottom line: Stick to major exchanges, never share your seed phrase with anyone, be deeply skeptical of unsolicited crypto opportunities, and remember: if you send crypto to a scammer, it's gone — transactions can't be reversed. The best defense is simple: slow down, verify everything, and if something feels off, walk away.
